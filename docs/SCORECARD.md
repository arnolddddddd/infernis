# Season scorecard — what the engine said the day before

**Every** qualifying fire in a fixed window. Not a selection of the ones that went well.

**This is an illustration, not evidence of predictive skill**, and the distinction matters. Nineteen
days in one province cannot establish skill. The measurements that can are pre-registered, adversarial
and reported in the engine's internal claims ledger — where fire skill over a free baseline is recorded
as **NOT ESTABLISHED** at both 5 km and 1 km. Nothing on this page changes that, and no sales material
may quote a number from here as accuracy.

It exists because *"what did you say the day before?"* is the first question anyone sensible asks,
and the honest answer includes the fires we missed.

## How it was built, in order

1. **The rule was written and committed first** — a frozen pre-registration, dated 2026-08-11 —
   before the analysis script ran, so the event set could not be chosen to suit a result.
2. Two amendments were made, both **before any recall number existed**, both recorded in that
   document rather than edited away: the event date is the attributed `ignition_date` rather than the
   first satellite detection, and the served grid does not exist for the first four days of the
   declared window.
3. The scorecard is regenerated by a committed audit script, never hand-edited.

**The comparison is rate-matched.** "We flagged N of the cells that burned" is meaningless alone — a
model that calls everything HIGH scores 100%. So when the engine served HIGH-or-above to *k*% of
British Columbia, the free-map comparator gets the top *k*% of cells by historical ignition count.
Both flag the same share of the province, and the question becomes *which* cells.

## The headline, stated the way it should be read

Across the 17 material fires in the coverage-restricted window, the engine had **22 of 85 burned
cells at HIGH or above the day before, while alerting on 3.5% of the province.**

**And that number is carried by a handful of events.** Only **4 of 17** fires got any hit at all;
one supplies 55% of them. For the three *major* fires, **1 of 3** — and that single fire supplies
**100%** of the hits. A pooled recall quoted without this is misleading, and this project has already
been caught by exactly that trap once at 1 km, where a single two-ignition day carried 85% of a
headline.

| | major (≥500 detections, ≥5 cells) | material (≥100 detections, ≥3 cells) |
|---|---|---|
| fires | 3 | 17 |
| burned cells | 31 | 85 |
| **engine** HIGH+ the day before | **12** (38.7%) | **22** (25.9%) |
| **free map**, rate-matched | **12** (38.7%) | **21** (24.7%) |
| fires with *any* engine hit | **1 of 3** | **4 of 17** |
| province-wide alert rate | 4.5% | 3.5% |
| verdict | indistinguishable | indistinguishable |

A one-cell difference across 85 is not a win, and is not reported as one.

## What this does and does not support

**Supports.** That the engine is operating daily across the province, that its output is recorded
before events rather than reconstructed after, that its ranking concentrates risk far above chance —
12 of 31 major-fire cells at a 4.5% alert rate — and that we will publish the misses.

**Does not support.** Any claim of skill, accuracy, or advantage over free public data. On this
window the engine and a free static ignition map are **indistinguishable**, which is exactly what the
pre-registered measurements found at much larger sample sizes. If you need a wildfire ranking for
British Columbia and nothing else, the free map is a reasonable choice and we will say so.

**What you are actually buying**, if you buy: a maintained daily surface over 41,857 cells with fire
weather computed to the Canadian standard, per-response provenance (`model_sha256`,
`serving_chain_sha256`, `grid_version`), typed errors, and an evidence ledger that records its own
negative results. Not a claim to out-predict the public baseline.

## Limits, stated plainly

- 19 days, one province, one season. Nothing here generalises.
- Detections on the ignition date mark *where fire was observed*, not where it started. A large
  fire's footprint includes cells that burned by spread, which an ignition ranking was never asked to
  predict. This inflates the denominator for big events and is **not** corrected.
- Ground truth is FIRMS VIIRS near-real-time plus BC incident records, with the known caveats of
  near-real-time satellite data.
- Cells the engine declined to score count as misses, never as exclusions.
